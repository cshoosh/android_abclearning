# Making play more interactive

Research checked on 1 October 2026. This is product guidance, not a claim that the app has been clinically tested or that screen activities replace play with a caregiver.

## What the guidance points to

- NAEYC's joint position says interactive media is useful when it is chosen intentionally and supports active, hands-on exploration. Its toddler guidance emphasizes exploration with an adult, language-rich conversation, and using digital images to connect with real things around the child.
- The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends playing apps together and asking children to explain what they are doing or learning. Co-play helps caregivers follow the child's interests and make connections beyond the screen.
- A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of early-childhood game-based learning found positive outcomes across several measured areas. Its study-level analysis also found that results varied by game type; its authors note limitations and heterogeneity. This supports trying varied short activities, not promising learning gains from this app.
- The evidence does not show that more motion, sounds, or rewards automatically improve learning. The interactions here use a clear prompt, a large direct action, brief feedback, repeat play, and optional spoken language. There are no timers, failure sounds, or score pressure.

## Changes in this MVP

- **Count & Feed:** each tap adds one visible food item, with counting spoken aloud and a small real-world counting prompt for caregivers.
- **Animal Homes:** children choose among large habitat cards; an incorrect choice gets a calm retry prompt, and a correct choice explains the match before moving on.
- **Memory Match:** six cards make three identical-animal pairs. A child can retry mismatches without losing points, and caregivers get a turn-taking question.
- All rounds run offline, use device speech only when voice is enabled, and award one local star/sticker per completed session.
