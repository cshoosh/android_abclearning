## Common prompt for animated character clips

Silent 16:9 landscape animation in the same bright, friendly 3D style as ABC Friends. Keep the same personified uppercase A in every clip: a clearly readable capital A with a warm sunny-yellow body, expressive eyes inside its triangular opening, a small smile, short arms and feet. A is the main character. Alfie the Alligator is its friendly, silent sidekick; use `assets/animals/alligator.png` as Alfie's appearance reference. Keep both characters' designs consistent across clips. Use gentle, simple movement, soft lighting, and pastel backgrounds. No dialogue, lip-synced speech, music, captions, logos, or extra alphabet letters.

Use the master still at `assets/video/letter-a-scenes/reference-a-character.png` as the visual reference for every following character clip. Keep the pastel sky-blue (`#E5F8FF`) upper background and warm-cream (`#FFF4D4`) lower ground consistent. For Scene 03, use `assets/video/letter-a-scenes/reference-a-apple.png` as the image-to-video start frame and `assets/video/letter-a-scenes/reference-apple.png` as the fruit design guide; ignore the original screen interface/text.

## Scene prompts and narration

### Scene 01 — 0–6 sec
**Teacher:** “Hello, little learners! Are you ready to meet a very special letter?”

**Prompt:** A cheerful uppercase A peeks into a bright pastel frame, smiles, then waves to the viewer. Keep A large and clearly readable.

### Scene 02 — 6–11 sec
**Teacher:** “Today, we’re learning uppercase A!”

**Prompt:** The same A stands proudly at the centre, gives a friendly pose, and gestures toward its own clear capital-A shape.

### Scene 03 — 11–16 sec
**Teacher:** “A is for apple. A says ‘ah!’”

**Prompt:** A holds a bright red apple and reacts happily. Keep A larger and more visually prominent than the apple.

### Scene 04 — 16–21 sec
**Teacher:** “Meet Alfie the Alligator, A’s alligator friend!”

**Prompt:** Alfie enters and stands beside A. They smile and wave together; A remains the main focus. Keep Alfie silent.

### Scene 05 — 21–25 sec
**Teacher:** “Let’s make an uppercase A!”

**Prompt:** A raises its arms and gets ready to trace, with its capital-A shape facing the viewer.

### Scene 06 — 25–35 sec
**Teacher:** “Start at the top. Slide down one side. Back to the top, slide down the other. Add a line across the middle.”

**Prompt:** Use genuine ABC Friends tracing-screen footage with uppercase A selected. Show the tracing strokes in the narrated order. Keep the real app screen intact; do not generate or alter its interface.

### Scene 07 — 35–39 sec
**Teacher:** “Fantastic work! You made an A!”

**Prompt:** The completed uppercase A glows gently. A smiles, and Alfie gives a happy clap. Add a few small celebratory sparkles.

### Scene 08 — 39–43 sec
**Teacher:** “Look at our happy A, ready to play!”

**Prompt:** A does one joyful bounce while Alfie looks on. Keep the shot focused on their friendly expressions and the readable A shape.

### Scene 09 — 43–47 sec
**Teacher:** “Can you find A on the screen?”

**Prompt:** Use genuine footage of the app’s Find the Letter activity. Show the A target clearly and let the characters wait expectantly; keep the real app interface unchanged.

### Scene 10 — 47–49 sec
**Teacher:** No dialogue — silent pause.

**Prompt:** A and Alfie wait quietly and look toward the viewer. Use only a subtle sparkle or small gesture; leave space for the child to answer.

### Scene 11 — 49–53 sec
**Teacher:** “Hooray! You found it!”

**Prompt:** A jumps with joy and Alfie claps. Add a short, gentle burst of stars. This is a separate celebration shot after the pause.

### Scene 12 — 53–57 sec
**Teacher:** “Trace, listen, and play with ABC Friends!”

**Prompt:** Use genuine ABC Friends footage of the A learning and tracing activities. Keep uppercase A prominent and show only real app screens.

### Scene 13 — 57–60 sec
**Teacher:** “Get ABC Friends on Google Play!”

**Prompt:** End card with the personified A and Alfie beside the official ABC Friends branding and Google Play badge. Add the real logo and badge during editing rather than generating text inside the video.
